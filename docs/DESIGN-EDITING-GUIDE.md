# Changing Your Website's Design: A Plain-Language Guide to Claude

A reference for Lorraine. This is the companion to
[Editing Your Website](EDITING-GUIDE.md). That guide covers your site's
**content**: the words, photos, articles, and links you edit in Keystatic. This
guide covers your site's **design**: colors, fonts, sizes, spacing, layout, and
anything else about how the site looks and behaves.

Design changes are made by talking to **Claude**, an AI assistant you reach
through an app on your Mac. The app is on your Mac, but the work happens
safely online: Claude works on your website's master copy in the cloud, and it
knows the site inside and out. You describe what you want in plain English.
Claude does the technical work, shows you the result on a private link, and
nothing touches your live site until you say so.

You will never see or touch code. If Claude ever shows you something that looks
like code, you can ignore it. The plain-English summary is the part for you.

## 1. Design or content? Picking the right tool

You have two tools, and each is best at one thing:

- **Keystatic** (at `lorraineklee.com/keystatic`) is for **content**: the words
  and pictures themselves.
- **Claude** (the app on your Mac) is for **design**: how everything looks.

Use this cheat sheet:

| I want to change...                                        | Use this  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| The words in a headline, paragraph, or button              | Keystatic |
| A photo, or a photo's description                          | Keystatic |
| Adding an article, testimonial, or press feature           | Keystatic |
| Where a button points (its link)                           | Keystatic |
| A branded short link                                       | Keystatic |
| A button's **color, shape, or size**                       | Claude    |
| The **fonts**, or how big text is                          | Claude    |
| **Colors** anywhere on the site                            | Claude    |
| **Spacing**: sections feeling cramped or too spread out    | Claude    |
| Rearranging or redesigning a section's **layout**          | Claude    |
| Adding a **brand new kind of section** to a page           | Claude    |
| The free course **popup**: wording, timing, on or off      | Claude    |
| The press logo strip on the home page                      | Claude    |
| Anything the content guide calls a "developer task"        | Claude    |

**Rule of thumb:** if it is the words or pictures themselves, use Keystatic.
If it is how things look or behave, ask Claude.

> **One important difference in timing.** A Keystatic edit goes live on its own
> about 60 seconds after you save. That is by design: your words are yours, and
> they publish immediately. A design change through Claude works differently:
> it is rehearsed first and only goes live **when you say so**. Section 6
> explains how and why.

> **Can Claude also edit content?** Yes, technically, but Keystatic is faster
> and instant for content, so keep using it for words and pictures. Claude is
> your design tool.

## 2. What you need (one-time setup)

You and Ryan set this up together once, on a screen share. After that it is all
in place and you never redo it. For the record, here is what "set up" means:

1. **The Claude app is installed on your Mac** and you are signed in with your
   own Claude account (this account has a paid plan on it, which is what powers
   the assistant).

2. **Claude is connected to your website.** Your website's master copy lives
   online, on a service called GitHub that was connected to your account
   during your original Keystatic setup. Claude works on that master copy
   directly, in the cloud. Nothing about your website is stored on your Mac,
   so there is nothing on your computer to look after, and even a brand new
   laptop someday just means installing the app and signing in again.

3. **You have done one test change together**, end to end, so you have seen
   the whole loop once before doing it alone.

If any of these ever stops being true (signed out and stuck, or your website
no longer shows up in the app), see
[section 8](#8-if-something-goes-wrong) for who to contact.

## 3. Making a design change, step by step

This is the everyday loop. It is the same every time.

1. **Open the Claude app** on your Mac.

2. **Choose your website.** The website appears in the app's project list, so
   this is one click.

3. **Start a new conversation** and type what you want changed, in plain
   English, the way you would tell a designer. Section 4 has real examples.

4. **Let Claude work.** You will see it thinking, looking through the site,
   and making edits. This can take a few minutes for bigger changes. You do
   not need to watch it: the work happens in the cloud, so you can switch to
   something else, or even close your laptop, and the finished result will be
   waiting when you come back.

5. **Approve if Claude checks with you.** Claude sometimes pauses to confirm
   before a bigger step. For normal website work, approving is fine. If a
   question ever seems odd or you do not understand it, ask Claude to explain
   it in plain words first. You can always say no.

6. **Read the summary and open the preview link.** When Claude finishes, it
   tells you what it changed in plain language and gives you a private link to
   see it. That link is covered in section 5.

**One change per conversation.** Give each design request its own conversation
("New conversation" in the app). It keeps things tidy: each conversation is one
job, with its own preview, and "undo that" clearly means undoing that one job.

## 4. How to ask for what you want

Talk about what you **see**, name the **page**, and say what you **want
instead**. You never need technical words. Real examples:

> "Make the teal buttons across the site a darker teal."

> "The headings on the About page feel small to me. Make them bigger."

> "Add more breathing room between the sections on the Speaking page. It feels
> cramped."

> "The testimonial cards look boxy. Round the corners a little."

After Claude shows you a preview, keep going in the same conversation, like a
back-and-forth with a designer:

> "A bit darker still."

> "Too much. Go halfway back."

> "I like it on the home page. Do the same on the Book page."

**Three tips that make every request go better:**

- **Not sure exactly what you want? Ask for options.** "Show me two or three
  versions of this and let me pick." Claude can set up a preview for each.
- **If you can point at an example, do.** "Like the buttons on [some site you
  admire]" or "more like how the Speaking page does it" gives Claude a target.
- **Say when something must stay the same.** "Change the color but keep the
  size" saves a round trip.

**What if I ask for something Claude thinks is a bad idea?** Claude knows the
site's design rules (for example, that certain sections appear on several pages
and must stay matching, and that colors need enough contrast to stay readable).
If your request would break something like that, Claude will say so and suggest
an alternative instead of silently doing harm. You still decide.

## 5. Checking your change: the preview link

When Claude finishes a change, it gives you a **preview link**: a private web
address showing your site with the change in place.

- **Nobody else sees it.** The link is not your live site and visitors cannot
  stumble onto it. Look at it as many times as you like.
- **Check it on your phone too.** Half your visitors are on phones. Open the
  same link on your phone, or just narrow the browser window on your Mac, and
  make sure the change looks right small as well as big.
- **Not right yet?** Say so in the same conversation and Claude adjusts. Each
  round gets a fresh preview.
- **Lost the link?** Ask: "give me the preview link again."

## 6. Going live: the practice site, then the live site

Your website exists in two copies. You have seen these names in the content
guide already:

- **The live site (Main):** lorraineklee.com, what the world sees.
- **The practice site (Dev):** a complete, private copy of the site where
  changes rehearse before going live.

When you are happy with a preview, going live is a two-step:

**Step 1: put it on the practice site.** Say:

> "I'm happy with it. Put it on the practice site."

Claude moves the change over and gives you the practice site's link. Open it.
You are now looking at your **whole website** with the change in place, not
just a preview of one change.

**Step 2: make it live.** When the practice site looks right, say:

> "Make it live."

Claude will confirm with you in plain language ("this updates the live
website, ready?"), then publish. About a minute later, refresh
lorraineklee.com and your change is there.

**Why two steps?** The practice site is your dress rehearsal. A change that
looked fine on its own can clash with something else once it is sitting in the
full site, and the practice site is where that shows up, instead of in front
of your visitors, your clients, and anyone Googling you that day. The one
extra sentence ("make it live") is the whole cost of that safety net.

The two-step has a bonus: changes can **pile up** on the practice site. Try a
few design tweaks over a week, look at them all together, and then say "make
it live" once to publish them as a set.

> **Remember the asymmetry from section 1:** your Keystatic content edits skip
> all this and go straight to the live site in about a minute. That is on
> purpose. Words publish instantly; design rehearses first.

## 7. The shortcut: going live right away

The two-step is the default, not a law. It is your website. When something is
small or urgent (say, a color that looks wrong and is bothering you right
now), you can skip the rehearsal:

> "Put this live right away. Skip the practice site."

Claude will still confirm once before publishing, then send it straight to the
live site.

**The honest trade-off:** you are giving up the dress rehearsal. Your preview
link showed the change working, so this is usually fine for small things, but
there is no safety stop between you and your visitors. A good habit:

- **Tiny and urgent** (a color, one button, fixing something that looks
  broken): the shortcut is fine.
- **Anything bigger** (layout changes, new sections, several changes at once):
  use the two-step.

When in doubt, two-step. It only costs a sentence.

## 8. If something goes wrong

First, the reassuring part: **the loop is built so you cannot wreck the site
by accident.**

- Nothing you do in a conversation touches the live site until you say "make
  it live," and Claude confirms with you even then.
- The live site's history is protected behind the scenes. Neither you nor
  Claude can delete the website or erase its past. If a change goes live and
  turns out wrong, it can always be undone.

What to do in each situation:

**You changed your mind before anything went live.** Say "cancel this change"
or just close the conversation and walk away. Abandoned changes sit harmlessly
and touch nothing.

**A change went live and looks wrong.** Tell Claude:

> "The change we just made live broke the way the home page looks. Undo it."

Claude restores the previous look. Then, in a fresh conversation, you can try
again a different way.

**The preview link will not open or shows an error.** Say "the preview link
isn't working, check it." Usually it is a build still finishing; Claude will
sort it out.

**Claude seems stuck or keeps failing at the same thing.** Do not keep asking
the same way over and over. Note what you asked for, start a brand new
conversation, and describe the goal fresh. If it fails twice, stop and get
help:

> **Who to contact:** Ryan, your developer.
> _[Add Ryan's preferred email or Slack here before sharing this doc.]_

**Something about the setup itself broke** (signed out of the app and stuck,
or your website no longer shows up in the app): same contact as above. These
are five-minute fixes for a developer and not something to puzzle through
alone.

## 9. The whole loop on one card

1. Open the Claude app, open the website project, new conversation.
2. Say what you want, the way you would tell a designer.
3. Open the preview link. Check it on your phone too.
4. Adjust until you like it ("a bit darker," "undo that").
5. "Put it on the practice site." Look at the practice site.
6. "Make it live." Confirm. Refresh lorraineklee.com a minute later.

The three sentences that run everything:

- **"Put it on the practice site."**
- **"Make it live."**
- **"Undo that."**

## 10. Quick glossary

- **Claude:** the AI assistant in the app on your Mac. Your design department.
- **The cloud:** where Claude actually does its work: on your website's
  master copy, which is stored online under your own account. Nothing about
  the website lives on your Mac.
- **Preview link:** a private address showing one change before it exists
  anywhere real. Only for your eyes.
- **Practice site (Dev):** the full, private rehearsal copy of your website.
  Changes wait here until you send them live.
- **Live site (Main):** lorraineklee.com. What visitors see.
- **Change request:** the behind-the-scenes package that carries a change from
  rehearsal to live. Same word the content guide uses. Claude manages these
  for you.
- **Keystatic:** the content editor, for words and pictures, at
  `lorraineklee.com/keystatic`. Covered by its own guide:
  [Editing Your Website](EDITING-GUIDE.md).
